Neuroplasticity & Memory Consolidation

The current understanding of memory consolidation relies heavily on the two-stage model, positing that memories are initially encoded in the hippocampus during wakefulness and subsequently transferred to the neocortex during sleep, specifically during slow-wave sleep (SWS).

auto_awesome AI Synthesis

Recent studies suggest this transfer isn't a simple offloading, but an active restructuring. The hippocampus acts as a temporary index, while the neocortex integrates the new information with existing semantic networks, strengthening the trace against decay.

However, the specific mechanisms underlying this trace transformation remain poorly understood.

Should we cite the Tonegawa lab's recent paper on engram cell allocation here?

We propose investigating the role of sharp-wave ripples (SPWs) in hippocampal-cortical communication. SPWs, prominent during SWS, are believed to coordinate the synchronous reactivation of neural ensembles involved in the initial experience.

Methodology Outline

  • In vivo electrophysiology targeting CA1 and medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) in freely moving rodents.
  • Optogenetic inhibition of SPWs during post-learning sleep periods.
  • Behavioral assessment using the novel object recognition task (24h retention).

By disrupting specific oscillatory patterns, we aim to establish a causal link between hippocampal replay events and long-term neocortical storage.
